## Q1: What is LLM

A **Large Language Model (LLM)** is a type of generative artificial intelligence built upon the **Transformer architecture** that uses deep learning techniques to understand, summarize, generate, and predict human language.

### Core Concepts
*   **Architecture:** LLMs rely on the **Transformer** model, specifically the **self-attention mechanism**, which allows the model to weigh the importance of different words in a sequence regardless of their distance from one another.
*   **Scale:** The "Large" refers to two factors: the massive volume of training data (petabytes of text) and the number of **parameters** (the internal weights learned during training, often numbering in the billions or trillions).
*   **Probabilistic Nature:** At their core, LLMs are **next-token predictors**. They calculate the probability distribution of the next token in a sequence based on the context provided by preceding tokens.

### Real-World Example
Consider the prompt: *"The capital of France is..."*
The model does not "know" the answer as a database query. Instead, it processes the input through layers of neural networks to calculate that the token "Paris" has the highest statistical probability of following the sequence, given the patterns observed during its pre-training phase.

```python
# Conceptual representation of next-token prediction
logits = model(input_ids) 
probabilities = softmax(logits)
next_token = sample(probabilities) 
```

### Summary
An LLM is a statistical engine trained to model the structure of human language. By mapping text into high-dimensional **vector embeddings**, it captures semantic relationships, enabling it to perform complex tasks like reasoning, coding, and translation without explicit programming for those specific domains. Its power lies in **emergent properties**—capabilities that appear only once the model reaches a sufficient scale of parameters and data.
